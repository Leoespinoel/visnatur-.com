    04 · The brand

    Style first. Never the other way round.

    Vis Naturæ is Latin for force of nature. It is a resort label for men and women in the register of Aimé Leon Dore, Vilebrequin and Ralph Lauren: swim, linen, knitwear, trousers, caps and sunglasses. The clothes earn the attention. The conservation is what people find when they look closer.

    • One taste lens.Every piece and every page passes through the founder's taste. Taste stays fixed. Style and range are allowed to move.

    • Made for somewhere.Clothes made for the sea and the sun borrow everything from the places they are worn in. Ours are made on an island in the Indian Ocean.

    So what's the hard part?

    05 · The real challenge

    Two things that usually cancel out.

    Good clothes and conservation have rarely sat together without one cheapening the other. Holding both, without the cause tainting the brand, is the whole job.

    The trap on one side. Lead with the cause and we become a charity with merchandise. Nobody buys from a charity for the cut of the shorts.

    The trap on the other. Bury it and the ten percent becomes a footnote nobody feels. The one thing that makes us different disappears.

    The line we walk

    • Product gets the front row, always.Every design, film and post is judged as clothing first. If it only works because of the mission, it does not go out.
    • Conservation is shown, not sold.The work and the people doing it, filmed well. No sad animals, no guilt, no asking.
    • One standard for both.The film about the reef gets the same director, grade and taste as the lookbook. The moment the conservation side looks cheaper, it taints everything next to it.
    • The number does the talking.Ten percent of the price, on every page, published every season. No campaign has to say it twice.
    NextWhy does this need to exist?

    06 · The problem

    The industry makes far more than it sells.

    Then it discounts, destroys or dumps the rest. We decided not to take part.

    We started with a question: what would a clothing brand look like if it refused to make anything nobody wanted? The answer was slower, and better. No stock. No sales. No seasons thrown away.

    The pieces are the ones we wanted and could not find. Swim shorts in a clean cut. Linen that is allowed to crease. Knitwear for the drive home. A hoodie, a cap and a pair of sunglasses cut for anyone.

    08 · Why no limit

    One of one was the romantic answer. Made to order is the honest one.

    We started as a pure one-of-one brand. At an average price of about €174, a single sale left €30 to €60 before the design had been paid for. Covering a studio that way needs fifty to four hundred designs a month, or average prices near €550. Neither is this brand.

    The pattern and the sampling are paid once per design. With no cap, every further order carries that cost further, instead of the design being retired just as it finds its buyers. It keeps the price where a great product should sit, and still nothing is made before it is ordered.

    10 · Where

    Made in Mauritius. By hand.

    An island in the Indian Ocean that has been making fine knitwear and tailoring for half a century. We work with small workshops we visit in person, and every piece is cut and sewn there after it is ordered.

    Express from Mauritius to Europe takes four to seven working days and costs us about €35 a parcel. A buyer pays at most €15, and nothing over €150. The difference is a cost we carry on purpose. It sits inside the margin targets.

    The making is the content. We show the process, not only the polished result.

    11 · The product rule

    Give people more than what they pay for.

    The photograph shows the outside only. The buyer discovers the rest on delivery. Inside the cap, inside the shirt, the waistband, the labels. None of it is photographed or listed before purchase, so every piece arrives as more than what was bought.

    Every block is refitted for its fabric. A shirt that moves from cotton to linen is cut again, not just re-sewn. This is the quality gap most brands leave open, and a made-to-order brand can own it.

    What a buyer gets

    • A piece made for them, in their sizeCut and sewn in Mauritius after they order, never taken from stock.
    • Details inside the garment the photographs never show
    • A free remake if the size is wrongWithin fourteen days of delivery.
    • Free repairs for life
    • No plastic in the packaging

    What we never do

    • Run a sale
    • Hold stock, or make a piece nobody asked for
    • Invent a shortage, or a countdown
    • Buy an advertisement, or ask anyone to post
    • Photograph the inside before it is bought

    15 · The pledge

    Ten percent of the price. Not the profit.

    Percent-of-profit pledges are easy to make and hard to check. Ours is on the sale price, before shipping and tax, shown on every product page, so anyone can do the sum. A €165 swim short sends €16.50 to conservation.

    Because the brand takes its name from the natural world, it pays rent.

    16 · The mechanism

    From a checkout to a coastline.

    Transparency is the product feature. If we cannot show it, we do not claim it.

    1. 01

      A piece is bought.

      Ten percent of the sale price is set aside at checkout. Before costs, before margin, before shipping and tax.

    2. 02

      Held separately.

      Pledge funds sit in their own account. Never working capital, never payroll.

    3. 03

      Pooled each season.

      Allocated across the three remits: ocean, forest, rivers and wetlands.

    4. 04

      Transferred to partners.

      Established, audited organisations in each area, named before the first transfer is made.

    5. 05

      Published openly.

      Every season: raised, transferred, outcomes. Shown on the conservation page, where anyone can check what was raised against what was transferred.

    17 · The ten percent

    No. It pays for itself.

    It does not come out of what is left. It does the work a marketing budget would, and it hands us the stories to tell.

    • It is priced in.The ten percent sits inside the price of every piece from day one, like the fabric. A cost we plan for, not money taken out at the end.
    • It is our marketing.Other brands pay to be noticed. The pledge is the reason people talk about us, share us and come back. It does the job ad money would.
    • It is our content.Every partner we fund is a place we can film. One place, one episode, and a run of clips cut from it. The seasonal numbers become posts in their own right.
    • It is the emotional transfer.The buyer is let into a world, not charged a line item. That is the difference between a pledge and a footnote.

    21 · Risks

    Six things, said out loud.

    Better said now than discovered six months in.

    • Risk 01 · the biggest one

      The maker's numbers come back high.

      A piece made one order at a time may cost more than the model assumes, and the pattern per design still has to be earned back. The margin targets fall with it.

    • Risk 02

      A design draws few orders.

      Every design pays for its pattern and sampling before the first order. One that few people buy may never earn that back.

    • Risk 03

      The product misses the bar.

      We call it great and the first piece is only good. The whole claim falls with it.

    • Risk 04

      Distance costs more than planned.

      Shipping loses €20 a parcel, duties surprise a buyer, a remake has to cross the ocean twice.

    • Risk 05

      The pledge loses trust.

      If we cannot show exactly where the money went, we become the problem this page describes.

    • Risk 06

      One founder, too many hats.

      Design, maker, site, films and the pledge all run through one person. Attention splits and the calendar slips.

    NextWhat do we do about them?

    22 · Mitigation

    One answer for each.

    • Answers 01

      Quote before launch, price after.

      Nothing opens until the three quotes are in. If the quotes say the price is wrong, the price moves before launch, and the page says so.

    • Answers 02

      Patterns only where they are earned.

      Twenty-four styles at launch, every colour made to order. Nothing is made before it is ordered, so a style that draws few orders costs a pattern, not stock. New patterns go to the styles that earn them.

    • Answers 03

      One piece at a time, no shortcuts.

      Refit every block per fabric. Release nothing until the sample holds up. The hidden details are the test: if the inside is not worth finding, it is not ready.

    • Answers 04

      Price the distance in.

      The €20 shipping gap sits inside the margin target. Duties are stated on every page. Sizing guidance and the remake policy are written to make the second crossing rare.

    • Answers 05

      Proof built in.

      Ten percent set aside at checkout, held in its own account, named partners before the first transfer, published every season.

    • Answers 06

      Partners carry their part.

      The workshop runs the making, the conservation partners run the projects and the press tells the story. The founder keeps the taste and the numbers. Until those partnerships are signed, the calendar is sized to one person and says so.
